# Story script: The Pearl Coast (draft v0.1)

Status: **draft**, written by the agents (Filip's instruction of 1 Nov 2026: draft our own until
historians are hired after funding). Historical claims follow docs/research/AGE_V_RESEARCH_PACK.md.

Rules applied:
- The Rawi is an invented storyteller. He never says more than three sentences at a time [GDD].
- Real people (the six leaders) have **no invented dialogue** [GDD section 10]. They appear through
  their abilities, their tools and Archive pages.
- No religious figures, no worship as a mechanic, no Quranic text [GDD section 7, Age II rules].
- Choices follow the GDD's risk rule: the riskier option pays 10 to 20% more on average [GDD section 12]. Averages are noted per choice; the Designer confirms them in the simulator once routes exist.
- The labour history of pearling (flag H1) is not dramatised until the advisor decides how.
- No "demand their return" choice for indebted divers (decision of 31 Oct 2026).

## 2. Routes and their twelve decision points

Four routes, 2 + 3 + 3 + 4 decision points. Rewards are relative to the route's base cargo.

### Route 1: Errand to the date gardens (short errand, land, 8 minutes)
1. **The early dates.** "The gardener will sell this year's dates now, or more if you wait for the
   last of the harvest." Safe: base cargo. Risky: +20% cargo, 1 in 8 chance the late batch spoils (half cargo). Average +11%.
2. **The salt flat.** "A shortcut crosses the sabkha. The crust can break under loaded camels."
   Safe: go around (no effect). Risky: route returns 25% faster, 1 in 6 chance of losing a quarter of the cargo. Average about +20% cargo per hour.

### Route 2: Caravan to the oasis market (day caravan, land, 2 hours)
1. **The heat.** "Rest at the well through the worst of the day, or push on." Safe: base. Risky:
   +18% cargo, 1 in 8 chance a camel goes lame (cargo -40%). Average +11%.
2. **The guide.** "A local guide knows the wells and the tracks, for a fee." Safe: pay 10% of
   expected cargo, no hazard. Risky: follow the old tracks, 1 in 6 chance of losing a day (route time +50%).
3. **The market.** "Sell in the morning at a steady price, or wait for the traders from the mountains
   in the evening." Safe: base price. Risky: +25% if the traders come (2 in 3), -15% if not. Average +12%.

### Route 3: Coastal run to the Island Harbour (day voyage, sea, 2 hours)
1. **Coast or open sea** [GDD section 12]. Coast: no cargo loss, 1 in 7 chance of reef damage to the
   hull (-20% on later voyages until repaired). Open sea: +higher reward, nearly 1 in 3 chance of losing
   half the cargo; hull safe.
2. **The reef channel.** "Wait for the high tide, or take the channel now." Safe: arrive later
   (route time +25%). Risky: arrive on time, 1 in 8 chance of hull damage.
3. **The harbour fee.** "Pay the harbour master and trade in the souq, or anchor outside and trade
   from the boat." Safe: pay 10% of cargo, Island Harbour relations up a little. Risky: keep the fee,
   relations down a little.

### Route 4: Season voyage to Bombay (season voyage, sea, 14 hours, sailing season only)
1. **The wind.** "Leave on the early wind, or wait a day for a steadier one." Safe: route time +7%.
   Risky: leave now, 1 in 6 chance of a slow passage (route time +30%). Slightly faster on average.
2. **Coast or open sea** [GDD section 12], as above; storm chance rises from 30% to 45% at the end of
   the season and a forecast appears.
3. **A storm on the horizon.** "Shelter in a cove for a day, or run before the wind." Safe: route
   time +25%. Risky: on time, 1 in 5 chance of losing a third of the cargo. Average about +17% cargo per hour. Ibn Majid's Book of
   Benefits can ignore this storm once a day [GDD].
4. **The dealers of Bombay.** "Sell to the first buyer who meets you at the quay, or visit the
   dealers in the market." Safe: base price now. Risky: price moves -20% to +40% (average +12%) and
   the coin arrives one hour later.

Historical note for review: Bombay as the main market for Gulf pearls in the late nineteenth and
early twentieth centuries is stated in the GDD (Age V); confirm with Heard-Bey or Hopper.

## 3. The diving season: event cards

One cycle in the slice: fit out, the dive, the town alone, homecoming [GDD Age V]. Events break up the
dive days. The nahham's song plays under each card.

| Event | Text | Choices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Storm on the horizon | "Clouds stack over the banks. The captain watches the water change colour." | Bring the catch home now (keep all). Dive one more day (+catch value, 1 in 3 chance of losing that day's take). |
| The large pearl | "A diver surfaces with an oyster heavier than the rest." | Open it on deck now (reveal grade). Keep it sealed for the pearl buyer (sell blind at a fixed good price). |
| A boat in trouble | "A neighbouring boat signals for help. Its mast is cracked." | Send men and rope (lose half a day, relations up with its home town). Sail on (no cost). |
| Short of water | "The water jars are low. The nearest fresh water is a day away." | Sail for water (lose a day). Ration (crew morale down, next dive day yields 10% less). |
| The pearl buyer's boat | "A tawwash comes alongside and offers to buy part of the catch now." | Sell a third now at a fair price. Keep everything for the homecoming. |
| A crew in debt | "After a poor day, a diver asks to have part of his debt forgiven." | Forgive part of it (costs coin, reputation and loyalty up). Keep the debt (no cost). |

Homecoming (the qaffal): "The boats are coming home. The whole town is on the shore." The player
chooses where to sell: the local pearl buyer now, or Bombay later at a higher, moving price [GDD].

## 4. The age change and the collapse preview

The three warnings [GDD section 13], spoken by the Rawi:
1. When pearl prices start to slide: "The buyers pay a little less each season. Watch how much of your
   wealth rests on one thing."
2. In the Chronicle chapter: "Far to the east, men have learned to grow pearls inside living oysters.
   Cheaper ones. Many buyers will not wait to see if ours are better."
3. Before the player confirms the change: "If you go on, the pearl market falls. Your boats will stand
   idle and your town must find new bread. Are you ready?"

End card of the slice: the town restyles into the 1930s; boats lie on the sand; the Rawi:
"Every age ends. This town will have to learn a new trade, and quickly. That story is still to come."
